# Worked Example: Raw Win → Polished STAR Story

This shows the target transformation. The raw entry comes straight out of a
career file (see the `career-foundation` skill) — plain, note-form, gaps
allowed. The polished version is what this skill should produce: connected
prose, honest about scope, no invented facts.

---

## Raw win entry (input)

```
### Northwind Logistics — Regional rollout of new dispatch system
**Capture status:** detail complete

- Situation: Dispatch was still done by phone and whiteboard across 6
  regional depots. Errors were common, drivers waited around, customers
  complained about ETAs.
- Task: I was asked to lead the rollout of a new dispatch software across
  all depots within one quarter, without disrupting daily operations.
- Action: Mapped current process at each depot first, found they all worked
  differently. Built a phased rollout plan, depot by depot, starting with
  the smallest as a pilot. Trained dispatchers directly, in person, rather
  than relying on the vendor's generic training deck. Set up a feedback
  loop so depot managers could flag issues fast.
- Result: All 6 depots live within the quarter. Missed pickups dropped
  about 30%. One depot manager who'd been the most resistant became the
  internal go-to for questions from other depots.
- Metrics: 30% drop in missed pickups, 6 depots, 1 quarter
- Skills demonstrated: change management, process mapping, training design
```

## Polished STAR story (output)

```
**Topic:** Northwind Logistics dispatch system rollout
**Goal:** Roll out new dispatch software across 6 regional depots in one
quarter without disrupting daily operations

**Situation**
Northwind's dispatch process was still running on phone calls and a
whiteboard across six regional depots. Errors were common, drivers sat
waiting for instructions, and customers were complaining about missed or
vague ETAs.

**Task**
I was asked to lead the rollout of new dispatch software across all six
depots within a single quarter, with no disruption to daily operations —
the trucks still had to run every day during the transition.

**Action**
I started by mapping how each depot actually worked, rather than assuming
they'd all adapt the same way — it turned out no two ran the process
identically. I built a phased rollout, depot by depot, starting with the
smallest as a pilot so early problems surfaced somewhere low-stakes. Rather
than leaning on the vendor's generic training deck, I trained dispatchers
in person at each site. I also set up a direct feedback loop so depot
managers could flag issues immediately instead of waiting for a scheduled
check-in.

**Result**
All six depots were live within the quarter. Missed pickups dropped by
about 30%. The depot manager who'd pushed back hardest at the start ended
up becoming the person other depots called with questions.

**Skills demonstrated:** change management, cross-site process mapping,
hands-on training design, stakeholder buy-in under a hard deadline
```

---

## What changed between raw and polished

- Fragmented notes became connected prose — but every fact in the polished
  version traces back to something in the raw entry. Nothing was added.
- A one-line **Goal** framing was added at the top — this is new, but it's
  a compression of what's already in Situation + Task, not new information.
- The most interesting single detail (the resistant depot manager becoming
  the go-to person) was kept intact rather than smoothed into generic
  "successful adoption" language — specific beats general.
- Length target: roughly 150-300 words total, short enough to tell out loud
  in an interview without notes, long enough to hold the real shape of what
  happened.
