# Worked Example: Career File Entry → CV Bullet

CV bullets are a different, more compressed register than STAR stories.
A STAR story is something you'd say out loud over 30-60 seconds. A CV
bullet is something a reader scans in 3-5 seconds. Same underlying facts,
very different shape.

---

## Case 1: A well-detailed win

### Input (from career file, capture status "detail complete" or "polished")

```
Situation: Dispatch was still done by phone and whiteboard across 6
regional depots. Errors were common, drivers waited around, customers
complained about ETAs.
Task: Lead the rollout of new dispatch software across all depots within
one quarter, without disrupting daily operations.
Action: Mapped current process at each depot, built a phased rollout
starting with the smallest depot as a pilot, trained dispatchers in
person, set up a fast feedback loop with depot managers.
Result: All 6 depots live within the quarter. Missed pickups dropped
about 30%.
Metrics: 30% drop in missed pickups, 6 depots, 1 quarter
```

### Output (CV bullet)

```
Led rollout of new dispatch software across 6 regional depots in one
quarter, reducing missed pickups by 30% through phased deployment and
in-person dispatcher training.
```

Notice what happened: Situation and Task collapsed into implicit context,
Action compressed to the one or two moves that mattered most, Result led
with the number. One line, one sentence, front-loaded with the strongest
verb and the clearest number.

**Optional second-line variant**, if the role needs more depth (e.g. it's
the person's most recent or most relevant role):
```
Led rollout of new dispatch software across 6 regional depots in one
quarter, reducing missed pickups by 30%.
- Piloted the rollout at the smallest depot first to surface issues before
  scaling, and trained dispatchers in person rather than relying on
  vendor materials.
```

## Case 2: A thin win (capture status "raw" or "raw, unconfirmed")

### Input

```
Situation: Not recalled.
Task: Built a partner engagement strategy including legal framework,
integration support, and training material.
Action: Not recalled in detail.
Result: CV currently states "increased reach and support for sales
goals" — placeholder, not a real result.
Metrics: None available.
```

### What NOT to do

```
Spearheaded a comprehensive partner engagement strategy, driving
significant improvements in reach and sales support. ✗
```
This takes a placeholder phrase and dresses it up further — it now reads
as confident and specific, but nothing underneath it actually is. This is
the single worst failure mode for a CV builder: manufacturing false
precision from vague source material.

### What to do instead

Two honest options, and the choice belongs to the person, not the skill:

**Option A — scope-only bullet**, honest about what's actually known:
```
Built partner engagement strategy including legal framework, integration
support, and training materials.
```
No invented result. Shorter and less impressive-sounding than a "full"
bullet, and that's fine — it's accurate.

**Option B — omit for now**, and flag it in Open Threads for a
`career-foundation` follow-up before it goes on a CV at all. Often the
better call if the role already has other, stronger bullets covering it
and this one isn't load-bearing.

Never manufacture a metric or outcome to make Option A read as complete.

---

## General compression rules

- Lead with a strong past-tense verb (Led, Built, Launched, Negotiated,
  Reduced, Grew) — not "Responsible for" or "Worked on."
- One sentence, occasionally two. If it needs a bulleted sub-point, that's
  a sign the role deserves more than one bullet, not that this bullet
  should run long.
- The number, when one exists, belongs in the first sentence — don't bury
  it in a trailing clause.
- Cut connective narrative ("which allowed us to," "this meant that") that
  a STAR story needs and a CV bullet doesn't.
- Older or less relevant roles often compress to a single scope-line
  rather than getting the full bullet treatment — see the CV builder's
  guidance on relevance-based selection.
